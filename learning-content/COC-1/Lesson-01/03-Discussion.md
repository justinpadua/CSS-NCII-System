1.	Types of Computer Hardware
 Input Devices
1.	Keyboard – used to enter letters, numbers, and commands.
2.	Mouse – used to control the pointer and select items on the screen.
3.	Scanner – converts printed documents into digital files.
4.	Microphone – records voice and sound.
5.	Webcam – captures images and videos.

Processing Devices
1.	CPU (Central Processing Unit) – executes instructions and processes data.
2.	GPU (Graphics Processing Unit) – processes graphics, videos, and visual information.
3.	Motherboard – holds and connects components and provides communication pathways; it is not a processing device like the CPU or GPU.

Storage Devices
1.	HDD – stores files using magnetic disks.
2.	SSD – stores files using flash memory and provides faster data access.
3.	USB Flash Drive – portable storage device used to transfer files.
4.	Memory Card – small storage device commonly used in cameras and mobile devices.


Output Devices
1.	Monitor – displays visual information.
2.	Printer – produces hard copies of documents.
3.	Speakers – produce audio output.
4.	Projector – displays computer output on a larger screen.


1.	Types of Computer Software
System Software

1.	Operating System – manages the computer's hardware and software resources.
2.	Device Drivers – allow hardware devices to communicate with the operating system. It helps the software to work and connect with hardware.
3.	Utility Software – maintains and protects the computer system.
4.	Firmware – provides low-level instructions for hardware operation. It controls and manages hardware devices.

 Application Software
1.	Microsoft Word – creates and edits documents.
2.	Microsoft Excel – performs calculations and organizes data.
3.	Google Chrome – accesses websites and online resources.
4.	Adobe Photoshop – edits and manipulates images.
5.	VLC Media Player – plays audio and video files.

1.	Occupational Health Standards (OHS) 
1.	Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) refers to the rules and practices that protect workers from accidents, injuries, and health hazards in the workplace.

A. Importance of OHS in Computer Servicing
1.	Prevents electrical shock and injuries.
2.	Protects computer components from electrostatic discharge.
3.	Maintains a clean and organized work area.
4.	Prevents fire hazards and equipment damage.
5.	Promotes safe and efficient computer servicing.

B. Common Workplace Hazards
Hazard	Description	Preventive Measure
Electrical Hazard	Risk of electric shock from electrical devices	Unplug the computer before servicing
ESD Hazard	Static electricity can damage sensitive parts	Use an ESD wrist strap and mat
Physical Hazard	Loose cables may cause tripping	Organize cables properly
Chemical Hazard	Cleaning chemicals may irritate skin	Use approved cleaners and follow labels
Ergonomic Hazard	Poor posture can cause body pain	Use proper workstation positioning
Fire Hazard	Overloaded electrical outlets may cause fire	Avoid overloading outlets

C. Safety Rules in Computer Servicing
1.	Switch off and unplug the computer before opening the system unit.
2.	Use an antistatic wrist strap connected appropriately to an ESD-safe grounding point.
3.	Never open the PSU casing because internal capacitors may retain dangerous voltage.
4.	Keep liquids away from computers and electrical equipment.
5.	Use the correct tool for each task.
6.	Do not force computer components into slots.
7.	Keep screws and small parts in containers.
8.	Ensure proper ventilation in the working area.
9.	Maintain a clean and dry workspace.
10.	Follow the manufacturer's instructions and workplace procedures.

1.5 Creating a Small Computer Shop
- A computer repair shop is a business that provides computer maintenance, troubleshooting, repair, installation, and upgrade services.

A. Basic Requirements of a Computer Shop
1.	Shop location – accessible to customers.
2.	Workbench – stable surface for computer repairs.
3.	Computer technician – trained person responsible for servicing.
4.	Tools and equipment – screwdrivers, ESD tools, diagnostic devices.
5.	Spare parts – RAM, SSD, cables, fans, and other components.
6.	Customer service area – space for receiving customers.
7.	Storage area – organized place for tools and spare parts.
8.	Electrical outlets – safe and properly installed power sources.
9.	Internet connection – needed for downloading drivers and software.
10.	Documentation – service forms, receipts, inventory, and customer records.

B. **Illustrative Sample Shop Layout – For Learning Purposes Only**

This example organizes the shop requirements listed above into practical work areas. It is one possible arrangement, not a mandatory layout.

- **Customer service area:** Receive customers, record reported problems, and keep service forms and customer records together.
- **Workbench and repair area:** Provide a stable, clear work surface for inspection and servicing. Keep only the computer and tools needed for the current task on the bench.
- **ESD handling area:** Use the instructor-approved antistatic mat and wrist-strap arrangement when handling sensitive components. Keep loose parts in suitable antistatic packaging when they are not being handled.
- **Tools and parts storage:** Keep tools and spare parts organized and labeled. Store them off the work surface when they are not in use.
- **Testing and documentation area:** Keep diagnostic equipment and service records available for checks, results, and reports.
- **Power and ventilation:** Arrange work so approved outlets and ventilation are accessible. Manage cables and keep walkways clear, following local laboratory procedures.

A possible workflow is customer intake, repair at the workbench, then testing and documentation. Tools and parts can be stored near the repair area without crowding it. The actual placement depends on the room and local procedures.

> This is an illustrative example, not an official TESDA-prescribed layout. Actual shop arrangements depend on available space, equipment, electrical requirements, safety rules, and local laboratory procedures.
