# COC 1 — Lesson 3: Disassembly and Assembly of Computer Hardware

## Activity

LESSON 3 – ACTIVITY: TAKE IT APART, PUT IT BACK

**Learning objective:** Sequence safe basic disassembly, identify component connections, and select reasonable first troubleshooting checks.

**Materials:** Worksheet and pen. Optional instructor-approved unplugged desktop, ESD-safe mat/wrist strap, and component/connector photographs. Paper-only completion is acceptable; no equipment is required.

Practice disassembly, assembly, and troubleshooting.

How to use: click each gray field in Microsoft Word to pick an answer from the drop-down list, tick a box, or type your response.

Part A – Order the Disassembly

Number the steps from 1 to 6 in the correct order. The first step includes shutdown and external power disconnection.

- Remove the side panel after power isolation and ESD preparation.
- Remove RAM and expansion cards only if the instructor authorizes removal; use their retention mechanisms and handle them by their edges.
- Wear a properly grounded antistatic wrist strap before handling components.
- Place each removed part and its screws in labeled ESD-safe storage.
- Take reference pictures, then disconnect internal cables carefully.
- Shut down the computer and disconnect its power cable and external cables.

Part B – Match the Part to its Slot

Choose what connects to each slot or connector.  Word bank: CPU cooler fan • Front audio jack • Graphics card • Main power from the PSU • Memory module • NVMe SSD

1. A memory stick installs in a long DIMM slot with a keyed notch.
2. A discrete video card installs in a compatible PCI Express expansion slot.
3. A cooler fan lead connects to the motherboard header labeled for CPU fan.
4. The broad motherboard power lead connects to the main ATX power socket.
5. A small gumstick-shaped storage drive secures in a compatible M.2 socket.
6. The case's audio lead connects to the motherboard front-audio header.

Part C – Diagnose It

Choose the first check for each symptom.  Word bank: Audio settings and driver • Monitor cable and RAM seating • Power cable, outlet, and PSU switch • Replace the case • Temperatures, power, and memory

1. The desktop shows no power indicators after it was moved.
2. Fans start, but the display remains blank after a hardware change.
3. The PC shuts down during a demanding task and feels unusually hot.
4. The speakers are connected, but no sound plays after a new OS installation.

Part D – Assembly Precautions

Tick every correct precaution.

Handle components by their edges

Use a grounded antistatic wrist strap

Align the RAM notch before pressing it in

Apply thermal paste only when the cooler does not have it pre-applied

Force the CPU into the socket

Touch the CPU pins to check them

Part E – Apply It

A computer will not start.

List the first three troubleshooting steps you would take.

**Steps:** Complete the worksheet first. If equipment is unavailable, submit paper responses only. For an optional demonstration, use only an instructor-approved, unplugged unit; verify power is disconnected, follow the lab ESD procedure, handle parts by their edges, use no force, and never open the PSU. Point out connectors without removing parts unless specifically authorized. For each symptom, state why the selected check comes first and identify one later check only if the first check passes.

**Expected output:** Ordered six-step sequence, six connector matches, four first-check selections with reasons, completed precaution checklist, and three safe troubleshooting steps.
