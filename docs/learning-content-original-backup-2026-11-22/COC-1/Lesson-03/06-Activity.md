# COC 1 — Lesson 3: Disassembly and Assembly of Computer Hardware

## Activity

LESSON 3 – ACTIVITY: TAKE IT APART, PUT IT BACK

Practice disassembly, assembly, and troubleshooting.

How to use: click each gray field in Microsoft Word to pick an answer from the drop-down list, tick a box, or type your response.

Part A – Order the Disassembly

Number the steps from 1 to 6 in the correct order.

Part B – Match the Part to its Slot

Choose what connects to each slot or connector.  Word bank: CPU cooler fan • Front audio jack • Graphics card • Main power from the PSU • Memory module • NVMe SSD

Part C – Diagnose It

Choose the first check for each symptom.  Word bank: Audio settings and driver • Monitor cable and RAM seating • Power cable, outlet, and PSU switch • Replace the case • Temperatures, power, and memory

Part D – Assembly Precautions

Tick every correct precaution.

Handle components by their edges

Use a grounded antistatic wrist strap

Align the RAM notch before pressing it in

Apply thermal paste only when the cooler does not have it pre-applied

Force the CPU into the socket

Touch the CPU pins to check them

Part E – Apply It

A computer will not start.

List the first three troubleshooting steps you would take.

Instructor Answer Key

Part A: "Remove the side panel" = 3; "Remove the RAM and expansion cards" = 5; "Wear a grounded antistatic wrist strap" = 2; "Store the parts and screws in labeled containers" = 6; "Take pictures, then disconnect the internal cables" = 4; "Shut down the computer and unplug the power cable" = 1

Part B: 1. Graphics card, 2. Memory module, 3. CPU cooler fan, 4. Main power from the PSU, 5. NVMe SSD

Part C: 1. Power cable, outlet, and PSU switch, 2. Monitor cable and RAM seating, 3. Temperatures, power, and memory, 4. Audio settings and driver

Part D: Check: Handle components by their edges; Use a grounded antistatic wrist strap; Align the RAM notch before pressing it in; Apply thermal paste only when the cooler does not have it pre-applied

Part E: Answers vary. Sample: identify the problem and ask the user; check the power cable, outlet, and PSU switch; test one possible cause at a time.
