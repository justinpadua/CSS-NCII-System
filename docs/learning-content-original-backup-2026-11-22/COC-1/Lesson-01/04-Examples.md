Occupational Health Standards (OHS)

Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) refers to the rules and practices that protect workers from accidents, injuries, and health hazards in the workplace.

A. Importance of OHS in Computer Servicing

Prevents electrical shock and injuries.

Protects computer components from electrostatic discharge.

Maintains a clean and organized work area.

Prevents fire hazards and equipment damage.

Promotes safe and efficient computer servicing.

B. Common Workplace Hazards

C. Safety Rules in Computer Servicing

Switch off and unplug the computer before opening the system unit.

Use an antistatic wrist strap connected appropriately to an ESD-safe grounding point.

Never open the PSU casing because internal capacitors may retain dangerous voltage.

Keep liquids away from computers and electrical equipment.

Use the correct tool for each task.

Do not force computer components into slots.

Keep screws and small parts in containers.

Ensure proper ventilation in the working area.

Maintain a clean and dry workspace.

Follow the manufacturer's instructions and workplace procedures.

D. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

PPE

Antistatic Wrist Strap - Helps prevent static electricity from damaging electronic components

ESD Mat - Provides a protected working surface for sensitive electronic parts

Safety Goggles - Protects the eyes from dust and small particles

Protective Gloves - Protects hands during appropriate cleaning or handling tasks

Dust Mask - Helps reduce inhalation of dust during cleaning

Introduce the needed Tools and equipment

Hand Tools

Phillips Screwdriver

- Used to loosen and tighten cross-shaped screws.

- Commonly used for computer cases, HDDs, SSDs, and motherboards.

Flathead Screwdriver

Used for screws with a straight slot.

May also be used to carefully release certain clips when appropriate.

Needle-Nose Pliers

Used to hold small components, wires, and connectors.

Useful in tight spaces.

Tweezers

Used to handle small screws, jumpers, and tiny parts.

Nut Driver

Used to tighten or loosen hexagonal nuts and standoffs.

B. Cleaning Tools

Anti-Static Brush

Removes dust from components without generating excessive static.

Used on fans, heat sinks, and circuit boards.

Compressed Air

Removes dust from hard-to-reach areas.

Hold fans still while cleaning to avoid overspinning.

Microfiber Cloth

Cleans monitor screens and computer surfaces.

Helps prevent scratches.

Isopropyl Alcohol (appropriate electronics-grade concentration)

Used to clean certain electronic surfaces and remove thermal paste.

Apply carefully with the device powered off and allow it to dry completely.

C. Testing and Diagnostic Equipment

- Digital Multimeter

Measures voltage, resistance, and current when used correctly.

Helps identify electrical faults.

- Power Supply Tester

Checks basic PSU output conditions.

Helps identify a faulty power supply.

- POST Diagnostic Card

Displays diagnostic codes during computer startup.

Helps identify possible motherboard or startup problems.

- Temperature Monitoring Software

Monitors CPU and GPU temperatures.

Helps detect overheating.

D. Installation Equipment

USB Flash Drive – used for bootable operating system installation media.

External HDD or SSD – used for backup and file transfer.

SATA Cable – connects SATA storage devices to the motherboard.

LAN Cable – connects computers to a wired network.

1.4 Business Costings (Repair, Installing, Reformatting, etc.)

- Business costing is the process of calculating the expenses and service charges involved in computer servicing.

A. Common Computer Servicing Services

Computer Cleaning - Removes dust from computer components

Hardware Repair - Diagnoses and repairs defective hardware

OS Installation - Diagnoses and repairs defective hardware

OS Reformatting - Erases and prepares a drive for a fresh installation

Driver Installation - Installs software needed for hardware operation

Application Installation - Installs programs such as office applications

RAM Upgrade - Adds or replaces RAM

SSD Upgrade - Replaces or adds storage to improve performance

Virus/Malware Removal - Detects and removes malicious software

Data Backup - Copies important files to a safe storage device

B. Factors Affecting Service Cost

Labor cost – payment for the technician's time and skills.

Parts cost – price of replacement components.

Operating expenses – electricity, rent, internet, and other business expenses.

Complexity – difficult repairs may require more time.

Warranty – service guarantees may affect pricing.

Profit margin – amount added to cover business profit.

1.5 Creating a Small Computer Shop

- A computer repair shop is a business that provides computer maintenance, troubleshooting, repair, installation, and upgrade services.

A. Basic Requirements of a Computer Shop

Shop location – accessible to customers.

Workbench – stable surface for computer repairs.

Computer technician – trained person responsible for servicing.

Tools and equipment – screwdrivers, ESD tools, diagnostic devices.

Spare parts – RAM, SSD, cables, fans, and other components.

Customer service area – space for receiving customers.

Storage area – organized place for tools and spare parts.

Electrical outlets – safe and properly installed power sources.

Internet connection – needed for downloading drivers and software.

Documentation – service forms, receipts, inventory, and customer records.
